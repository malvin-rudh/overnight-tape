Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Inflation rises when spending financed by income and credit outruns the quantity sold. If bond returns fail to keep up with prices, gold gives up less buying power relative to holding bonds.

What the data say: Three forces matter. Payrolls rose by 165,000, supporting income and spending. The 10-year real yield rose to 2.66% on Monday, increasing the cost of forgoing bond income. Monday’s breakeven eased to 2.22%, suggesting less inflation compensation. Unlike a debt-monetisation episode with falling real returns, the latest available real yield rose. The dollar and oil moves help explain why gold could rise anyway.

What looks unsustainable: Debt that persistently grows faster than revenue leaves less room for other spending. Spending cuts, restructuring, transfers and monetary expansion can each shift the burden in different ways.

What would change this read: US CPI at 21:30 HKT Tuesday could move inflation expectations and yields. A sustained decline in real yields would strengthen gold’s monetary channel.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. If debt pressures eventually induce monetary easing and reduce those returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds also depend on inflation and repayment terms; nominal coupons do not guarantee buying power. Stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs, which the current labour and output data alone cannot settle.

AI and tech

Anthropic gives one model two sets of safeguards

An AI model can be offered with different rules about who can use it and what it can help with. Think of the same workshop tool being available under different safety rules. Anthropic introduced Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 on November 9. They share the same underlying model but use different safeguards. Fable is broadly available across Claude platforms, while Mythos is limited to verified organisations in cybersecurity and biomedical research. Anthropic says the new models are up to 30% faster than the previous 5.1 generation. Actual speed depends on the task, and the speed claim leaves open how reliably the models perform in those fields.

Sources: Company announcement · 9 Nov

Google splits its AI chips by workload

When an AI model answers a question about a long document, the computer needs to keep a lot of material close at hand. Think of working at a bigger desk so more pages stay within reach. Google announced two specialised versions of its TPU 8 hardware on November 9. The 8i Max gives each rack of computers more memory for jobs that use long documents or other large inputs. The 8tx uses encryption, which scrambles data to protect it, with features aimed at keeping sensitive information protected during processing. How either system performs on a team’s own tasks still needs to be measured.

Sources: Company announcement · Reuters via Euronext · 9 Nov
