Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: When spending financed by money and credit grows faster than the amount sold, prices rise. Gold pays no interest, so higher after-inflation bond yields increase the return an investor gives up by holding it.

What the data say: Three forces matter. First, producer prices rose 0.3% in October, down from a revised 1.4% in September, easing the monthly pace. The BLS released the data on 13 Nov. Second, the 10-year real yield rose to 2.91% on 13 Nov, increasing the return forgone by holding gold. Third, 10-year inflation compensation held at 2.24% on 13 Nov. In an inflation shock, the rise in bond yields can lag inflation expectations. Friday’s real yield climbed as inflation compensation held steady.

What looks unsustainable: If federal debt keeps growing faster than revenue, servicing it absorbs money that could go to other spending. Cuts, restructuring, transfers and monetary expansion spread the adjustment differently.

What would change this read: The first COMEX gold futures quotes after Monday’s 07:00 HKT reopening will show how the market absorbs the weekend headlines. A move sustained as US bonds reopen would help show whether the rates pressure has changed.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. Cash earns short-term interest and loses buying power if prices rise faster. If debt pressure leads to easing that cuts real returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds depend on inflation and repayment terms, while stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs.

AI and tech

Microsoft restores services after global outage

When a service like Outlook opens an inbox, its computers have to exchange information over a network. A problem in that network can disrupt several services at once. Microsoft said its services were fully restored Saturday after a global outage that began Thursday US time and continued into Friday. Outlook, Teams and Copilot were affected, and OpenAI and Anthropic reported trouble connecting to Microsoft 365. Microsoft traced the outage to a technical issue introduced during an earlier upgrade intended to let its network handle more traffic. The problem surfaced after a later network change. The total number of users affected remains unclear.

Sources: AP · 13–14 Nov coverage

Reuters traces Chinese AI projects that call for restricted Nvidia chips

Building a computing site involves paying for the building and power as well as the chips that do the calculations. Reuters reviewed more than 150 Chinese notices, plans, contracts and disclosures and reported on November 13 that it found more than US$3 billion in computing projects and equipment agreements built around restricted Nvidia processors. One proposed site in Fujian specified Nvidia H100 chips and was seeking partners. The total combines planned and completed projects and can cover buildings, power and equipment, so chip spending and the source of the processors remain unclear.

Sources: Reuters investigation · Reuters project details · 13–14 Nov coverage

Google expands Gemini 4 access with Hydrogen

Everyday coding can involve a series of small jobs, such as writing a function and running a test to see whether it behaves as expected. Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12 and updated the announcement on November 13. It designed Hydrogen to finish routine coding tasks quickly. The model is available in the Gemini app, in Google Search and through the Gemini API, which lets developers call it from their own software. Google says Carbon and Argon are aimed at tasks that require deeper reasoning. How accurate Hydrogen will be on a difficult coding problem still depends on the task.
