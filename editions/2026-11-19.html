Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Prices rise when money and credit spent grow faster than output. Gold pays no interest. Falling after-inflation bond returns reduce the income sacrificed by holding it.

What the data say: Three forces frame the read. First, the real yield slipped to 2.88% on 18 Nov, a small supportive move. Second, inflation compensation fell to 2.19%; nominal yields fell enough to keep real returns lower too. Third, the broad dollar index remained 122.6503 as of 13 Nov, too stale to confirm Wednesday’s currency move. The usual lower-real-return support did not explain the gold selloff. Reuters’ report of dollar and tightening pressure supplies a competing channel.

What looks unsustainable: Debt cannot indefinitely outgrow revenue without adjustment. Cuts, restructuring, transfers or monetary expansion distribute the cost differently.

What would change this read: US claims and Philadelphia Fed data at 21:30 HKT Thursday could shift growth and rate expectations. Their effect on after-inflation yields would test whether gold’s interest-income headwind is easing.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. Cash earns short-term interest and loses buying power if prices rise faster. If debt pressure leads to easing that cuts real returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds depend on inflation and repayment terms, while stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs.

Google rolls out Gemini 4 Neon

An AI model reads a request and produces an answer, with developers paying for how much text goes in and comes out. Google announced Gemini 4 Neon on November 18. It lists prices of US$3 per million input tokens and US$15 per million output tokens, the pieces of text a model reads or writes. Neon can consider up to one million of those pieces together. Google says it is rolling out through its developer services and to eligible paid users in Gemini and Google Antigravity. The company reports fewer fabricated answers in internal tests, while reliability on a user’s particular task remains unproven.

Sources: Google announcement · 18 Nov news
