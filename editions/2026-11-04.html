Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Gold competes with cash and bonds as a store of wealth. When a Treasury pays more after inflation, holding a metal that pays nothing costs more, so money leaves gold; when real returns fall or money is printed, it comes back. The template: late in a debt cycle, tight money squeezes borrowers until policy makers ease and devalue.

What the data say: Three forces. One, real yields: the 10-year real yield rose to 2.65%, a solid return after inflation. Two, tight money: openings of 7.52M and CPI of 3.7% keep hikes priced. Three, the debt load (Long view) argues for easing later. Today the first two dominate.

What looks unsustainable: Interest takes 21.9% of federal revenue while the Fed is still tightening. Like a family whose card interest grows faster than its pay, that only ends with spending cuts, more income, or cheaper money. How today differs: inflation is too high for the easing step.

Where this sits in the cycle: The gauges point to a late, highly indebted phase. Debt rose to 6.52 times a year's revenue, and the cushion of growth over the 10-year yield shrank to 0.69pp from 3.29pp a year ago, so debt is closer to growing faster than the income that services it. Foreign demand for Treasuries has slowed while the Fed's balance sheet is growing again. These are rough indicators, and one quarter of growth is noisy.

What the framework implies for asset classes: In this framework, while real yields are positive and money tight, cash and bonds pay a real return and gold struggles. If yields rise above growth, interest costs outrun income, and policy makers have historically chosen easier money and a weaker currency, conditions the framework links to gold and real assets beating bonds. Stocks depend on productivity against the cost of financing.

AI and tech

AMD's data centre chips nearly double in a year

A data centre is a warehouse of computers, and AMD sells two kinds of brains for them: server CPUs, which run general tasks, and GPUs, which do the arithmetic AI needs. AMD's Data Center revenue was $8.5 billion in its third quarter, up 97% from a year earlier, driven by EPYC server CPUs and the launch of Instinct MI450 GPUs. Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure are running workloads on the first Helios systems, which pair MI450 GPUs with EPYC CPUs. AMD expects about $17 billion next quarter. What is unknown is how much of that depends on a few big customers, since the release gives no split.

Sources: AMD 8-K · StockTitan · CNBC

Arista's network gear sales jump 58.5%

Thousands of AI chips are only useful if they can swap data quickly, so something has to connect them. Arista makes the network switches that link servers inside data centres, a bit like the junctions in a road system. It reported third-quarter revenue of about $3.66 billion, up 58.5% from a year earlier and above its own guidance of $3.3 billion, which it called its fastest year-over-year growth since its IPO. Shares rose about 13% after hours, after AI-linked stocks had pulled back in recent weeks over worries about slowing data-centre spending. Not confirmed: margins, profit and next-quarter guidance, so whether this pace lasts is an open question.

Sources: Arista 8-K · StockTitan · CNBC

Anthropic's IPO filing warns of AI that speeds up its own research

Imagine a carpenter who builds better tools, then uses those tools to build even better ones, faster each round. Recursive self-improvement is that loop with AI: a system good enough at building AI that progress speeds up sharply. Anthropic's revised IPO filing says it already uses AI in its own research, and that this could pose a considerable safety risk if it sharply accelerates progress. Its internal estimates for future models suggest it may soon meet its own threshold for dramatically accelerated progress. No current model has crossed it, but Anthropic admits it may only notice an acceleration after it has happened. The filing gives no timeline and names no models, and skeptics say such warnings also work as marketing.

Sources: TechCrunch (single source for the filing text)
