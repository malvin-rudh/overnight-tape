Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: A price is the money spent divided by the quantity sold. When spending outgrows output, cash loses buying power and gold, a rival store of wealth, gains, unless bonds pay more than inflation.

What the data say: Three forces. First, the real yield, the return after inflation, is 2.63%: bonds still pay well, which works against gold. Second, inflation is sticky, with core PCE at 3.1% and CPI at 3.7%, while 3.4% real growth gives the Fed room to stay tight. Third, hiring is weak, at +29k payrolls and 4.2% unemployment, which caps how far tightening can go. The template says tight real money hurts gold, and Thursday fit it. The difference: real yields fell 4bp and gold still slipped, so positioning the template leaves out may be at work. Rough gauges only.

What looks unsustainable: Interest takes 21.9% of federal revenue and debt is 6.52 times revenue, like a family whose interest bill swallows a fifth of its pay.

What would change this read: A soft Q3 Employment Cost Index at 20:30 HKT today (BLS; 0.8% q/q forecast, Forex Factory, 30 Oct), or weak October payrolls on Fri 6 Nov.

Where this sits in the cycle: Debt keeps growing faster than the revenue that services it, 6.52 times revenue against 6.40 a year ago, which fits a late, highly indebted phase. The new Q3 growth data narrowed the gap between borrowing costs and growth sharply: the 10-year now sits only 0.55 points below nominal growth, against 3.41 points a year ago. As that cushion thins, debt stops shrinking relative to income. These are rough, noisy gauges, not timing signals.

What the framework implies for asset classes: In this framework, while real yields stay positive and money is tight, cash and bonds pay a real return and gold struggles. If rising debt service pushes policy makers toward easing or money creation, as the Fed balance sheet turning from -6.3% to +2.4% a year hints, cash and bonds tend to lose buying power and gold tends to gain. Stocks depend more on productivity and earnings.

AI and tech

Amazon's cloud speeds up, but the spending bill spooks investors

AWS is Amazon's business of renting out computing power and storage over the internet, like a power company selling electricity by the hour. In the third quarter of 2026 it sold $46.0 billion, up 39% from $33.0 billion, and kept 38.7% of that as operating profit. So why did the shares drop after hours? The bill. Capital spending, meaning money put into property and equipment, hit $77.3 billion in the quarter against $35.1 billion a year earlier, and over twelve months more cash went out than came in. Those spending figures come from a single source. Whether the AI data-centre spending will earn back its cost is unknown, and the CEO's forecast of over 40% AWS growth in 2027 and 2028 is a forecast, not a result.

Sources: StockTitan · Motley Fool via Yahoo Finance

Intel's AI server chips grow 78%, but a PC slowdown sinks the shares

A server is a computer that other computers connect to, while a PC is the one on your desk. Intel's third-quarter 2026 numbers show those two worlds pulling apart. Data Center and AI chips brought in $7.3 billion, up 78%, while PC chips brought in $8.1 billion, down 5%. Management expects PC softness to last through at least the first half of 2027 and pointed to robust demand for its Xeon server processors. Revenue of $16.5 billion landed just under the $16.6 billion estimate, yet adjusted profit per share of $0.52 beat the $0.41 estimate. Shares still fell 16.82% after hours, but thin after-hours trading can change before the open, and the fourth-quarter guidance figures were not in the sources.

Sources: MoneyCheck · MarketScreener

Google gives its new code-security model to defenders first

A benchmark is just a standard exam for a model: the same questions for everyone, scored afterward. Google's Gemini 4 Carbon reports 88.2% on finding software vulnerabilities, 54.8% on exploiting them and 71.0% on patching them. Notice who graded it: Google. These are vendor-reported scores, run without guardrails, and the safety claims come from internal testing, with no independent tests in the sources. The notable choice is access. Starting this week only a subset of cyber defenders can use it, expanding gradually to trusted partners, while developers, businesses and consumers wait. Launch pricing is $2 per million input tokens, a token being a chunk of text, rising to $4 after the introductory period. Whether defenders-first actually helps defenders is unproven.

Sources: Google · The Neuron
