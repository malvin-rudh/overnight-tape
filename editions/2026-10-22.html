Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Gold competes with cash and bonds as a store of wealth. Holding it costs the real yield forgone, so it gains when inflation outruns yields or money is printed, and loses when real yields rise and money stays tight.

What the data say: Three forces. First, the real yield of 2.76% is barely below 2.77%, still a high cost of holding gold. Second, CPI at 3.7%, up from 3.4%, erodes the buying power of cash. Third, payrolls of +29k signal slowing. The usual template has slowing growth bring easier money, which helps gold. Today differs: inflation, not growth, sets policy, so a slowdown arrives with hike pricing rather than cuts.

What looks unsustainable: Borrowing at 5.13% for ten years while hiring stalls, like a family whose loan rate climbs as its pay stops rising. These are rough gauges, not a timing tool.

What would change this read: Jobless claims at 20:30 HKT Thu 22 Oct; US flash PMIs at 21:45 HKT Fri 23 Oct; the FOMC decision at 02:00 HKT Thu 29 Oct.

AI and tech

AI-found PHP flaws push sites offline

PHP is a programming language that builds many web pages on the server when they load. Two bugs in PHP, present in every version since PHP 5 in 2004, let a stranger run commands on a server without logging in, which is what 'unauthenticated remote code execution' means. Think of a shop door that opens for anyone who knows the right knock. AI tools found them; emergency fixes shipped on 21 Oct, and agencies told organisations unable to patch quickly to go offline. Unproven is the scale: Wiz says roughly 70% of the internet is affected, the BBC cannot verify that, and whether any attack has succeeded is disputed.

Sources: BBC · The Register · TechCrunch · The Verge

Meta beats, adds non-voting stock, faces $25bn penalty

Meta's quarter holds three separate stories. First, revenue rose 26% to $64.60bn, but costs rose 68%, so operating margin fell from 40% to 20%; part of that is a $9.89bn charge for a teen mental health settlement. Second, a new non-voting Class C stock arrives on 19 Jan 2027, handed free to every current shareholder. Meta says it is for capital raising, employee pay and acquisitions while keeping Zuckerberg's founder control. Why does that work? Extra shares can be issued without extra votes. Third, a New Mexico judge ordered $25bn in penalties, which Meta will appeal. Unknown: whether Meta will book that as a charge.

Sources: Meta filing · TechCrunch · BBC

Tesla profit falls 43% as Optimus production starts

Tesla's profit fell while its robot news grew. GAAP net income was $787m, down 43%, on revenue of $28.046bn, flat against last year, and deliveries fell 2%. Meanwhile Optimus, a humanoid robot, has started production on high-volume lines in Fremont, though Musk himself called it 'early production'. The claimed rate of 20,000 per week by end-2027 is a promise from Musk, reported by one source, not a result. Also unproven: the timeline for unsupervised self-driving slipped to 'somewhere around Q2 next year' for the first customer cars joining the robotaxi fleet. Whether revenue beat depends on whose consensus is used.

Sources: Tesla filing · TechCrunch · The Verge

Lam and TI post strong quarters; Nvidia shows a factory-built pod

Why would a company that makes chipmaking machines do well when people want AI? Every AI chip must first be manufactured. Lam Research posted record revenue of $8.22bn, up from $5.32bn a year earlier, and its CEO credited AI-driven demand; Texas Instruments reported revenue up 28% to $6.06bn. Nvidia showed the Vera Rubin DSX Pod, a datacenter built in a factory as a container, due early 2027; Huang claims it cuts setup from 4 to 5 months to about one, which is unproven so far and comes from a single report. Lam's shares reportedly slid despite the beat, by an unknown amount.
