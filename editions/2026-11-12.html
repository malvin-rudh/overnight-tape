Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: When spending financed by money and credit grows faster than the goods and services sold, prices rise. Gold pays no interest, so higher bond yields after inflation increase the cost of holding it.

What the data say: Three forces matter. October consumer prices rose 0.4% on the month, keeping pressure on policy. The last cash-market real yield was 2.78% on 10 Nov, which raised the return investors give up when holding gold. Ten-year breakeven inflation was 2.20% on 10 Nov, below its prior reading. In a debt-monetisation episode real returns can fall as prices outrun yields. Here real yields rose in the last open Treasury session, while Wednesday’s gold decline coincided with a stronger dollar.

What looks unsustainable: Persistent debt growth above revenue growth squeezes future spending. Spending cuts, restructuring, transfers and monetary expansion can each shift the cost of adjustment.

What would change this read: US producer prices at 21:30 HKT Friday could change expectations for inflation. Reopened Treasury trading will show whether real yields hold Tuesday’s increase.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. If debt pressures eventually induce monetary easing and reduce those returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds also depend on inflation and repayment terms; nominal coupons do not guarantee buying power. Stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs, which the current labour and output data alone cannot settle.

CoreWeave raises its forecast as AI stocks rebound

CoreWeave rents access to AI chips over the internet. A team can pay to run a model on CoreWeave’s computers instead of buying the equipment itself. The company reported a US$709 million third quarter net loss on November 10 and raised its 2026 revenue forecast to US$13.0 billion to US$13.3 billion. It expects about US$9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months. AP reported shares rose 24.7% on November 11 after CoreWeave’s loss per share came in smaller than analysts expected. The deposits would bring in cash if received, and profitability still depends on whether revenue exceeds costs.

Sources: CoreWeave results · AP market reaction · 10–11 Nov

Google expands access to Gemini 4 Carbon

Some coding tasks take several attempts. Fixing a bug, for example, can involve checking where it starts, trying a change and testing whether it works. Google designed Gemini 4 Carbon for extended work of this kind in coding, debugging and research. Compared with Argon, Carbon uses more tokens, the small units of text a model processes, as it explores possibilities and checks its work. Google broadened access on November 10 after previously offering Carbon to selected cybersecurity defenders. Developers can now use it through Google’s paid service, and it is rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers. The extra processing can still produce an incorrect answer.

Sources: Google rollout announcement · VentureBeat · 10–11 Nov
