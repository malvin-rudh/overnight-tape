Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Prices rise when money and credit spent outgrow output. Gold pays no interest, so its attraction depends partly on the after-inflation return available from bonds.

What the data say: Three forces matter. First, the real yield stayed at 2.88% on 19 Nov, leaving gold’s real-income disadvantage intact. Second, inflation compensation fell to 2.16%. Nominal yields fell, but this time real returns did not. Third, claims rose to 203,000 from a revised 202,000, a small labour-market softening rather than evidence of a sudden collapse. The usual support from lower real yields was absent; Reuters instead reported currency and tightening pressure.

What looks unsustainable: Debt cannot keep outgrowing revenue without adjustment. Cuts, restructuring, transfers or monetary expansion shift the cost among groups.

What would change this read: Fed Governor Cook’s remarks at 01:15 HKT Saturday could clarify how policymakers weigh inflation against softer activity. A change in expected policy and real yields would alter the gold read.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. Cash earns short-term interest and loses buying power if prices rise faster. If debt pressure leads to easing that cuts real returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds depend on inflation and repayment terms, while stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs.

AI and tech

Anthropic rises on debut while other AI shares fall

A public share offering sells small ownership stakes in a business to investors. Once trading begins, buyers and sellers set a market price for those shares. Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq as ANT on November 19. The company had priced its offering at US$83 per share the previous day. Its first trade was US$111.50, and it closed at US$97.17, about 17% above the offer price. Think of those as three price tags at three different moments. The company confirmed 980 million shares in the offering. Whether Anthropic can turn its business into sustained profits remains an open question.

Sources: Anthropic · Investing.com · 19 Nov debut; 18 Nov pricing background

Amazon connects AI proof writing to a computer checker

A mathematical proof needs each logical step to follow from its starting assumptions. For example, a convincing explanation of a calculation can still hide a mistaken step. Amazon researchers described tools on November 18 that connect AI agents to Isabelle, a proof assistant that checks arguments written in precise mathematical language. As the AI develops an argument, the computer can check those steps. Amazon presents the tools as help for mathematicians, with hard problems still needing human thought. The checks depend on the exact statements and assumptions supplied, leaving open whether those capture the problem someone actually intended to solve.
