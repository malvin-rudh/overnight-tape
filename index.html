Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Gold competes with cash and bonds as a store of wealth but pays nothing. When the after-inflation return on bonds rises, the cost of holding gold rises with it. When inflation looks set to outrun yields, gold gains. A price is the money spent divided by the quantity sold, so an oil shock lifts measured inflation without adding any output.

What the data say: Three forces. First, the real yield rose to 2.62%, a high bar for gold. Second, inflation is still above target, with CPI at 3.7% and core PCE at 3.1%, but expected inflation is easing: the breakeven fell to 2.29% as oil dropped. Third, money is staying tight while jobs slow. Payrolls rose only 29k, yet the factory PMI reached 55.5. The template is a tight-money squeeze. Today gold rose anyway, on cheaper oil.

What looks unsustainable: Interest takes 21.9% of federal revenue, like a family whose loan payments eat a fifth of its pay. The cushion between growth and the 10-year yield has thinned to 0.66pp.

What would change this read: Midterm results from about Wed 07:00 HKT, JOLTS at Tue 23:00 HKT and October payrolls at Fri 21:30 HKT.

Where this sits in the cycle: Debt is still growing faster than the revenue that services it: it now equals 6.52 years of revenue, up from 6.40. Over the year the 10-year yield climbed most of the way to nominal growth, and the gap narrowed to -0.66pp from -3.29pp. That gap is the cushion that has kept the debt burden manageable, and it is nearly gone. Foreign demand for Treasuries has slowed, and the Fed's balance sheet is growing again. Together these readings point to a late, highly indebted phase, though they are rough indicators, not a timer.

What the framework implies for asset classes: In this framework, positive real yields reward cash and bonds while money stays tight, which is a headwind for gold. If the cushion vanishes, the usual escape is easier money and a weaker currency. That pattern has historically favoured gold and real assets over nominal bonds. Stocks depend on whether productivity outpaces rising financing costs.

AI and tech

Marvell agrees to buy Lumentum for $115 billion in stock

In an all-stock deal, the buyer pays with its own shares: each Lumentum share becomes 4.500 Marvell shares, like swapping one collectible card for four and a half of another. That implies about $115 billion including debt, roughly $1,132 a share, a 23.9% premium to the 28 October close. Afterwards Marvell holders own about 67% and Lumentum holders about 33%. The companies claim over $1 billion of yearly savings and a $550 billion combined market, pitched as an AI data-centre bet. Investors were unimpressed on Monday: Lumentum fell about 18%, Marvell about 13%. Is Marvell overpaying? An IG analyst asked that, and regulators must still approve before closing in the first half of 2028.

Sources: Joint release (SEC) · Reuters · CNBC

Anthropic's updated IPO filing shows $33 billion of revenue

An S-1 is the filing a company makes with the SEC before listing shares, a first public look at its books. Anthropic's amended one shows revenue of $33.24 billion for the nine months to 30 September, about $17 billion of it in the third quarter. It also shows a net loss of $104 billion, which CNBC says comes mostly from accounting changes in the value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities rather than operations. Separately, about $895.4 billion of purchase commitments and leases are listed, substantially all for compute and infrastructure. Those last two figures rest on CNBC alone, no price range or ticker exists yet, and growth rates differ by outlet.

Sources: CNBC · Yahoo Finance

Oracle raises $20 billion in stock and bonds for AI

Companies fund big projects by borrowing or by selling new slices of themselves. Oracle's filing does both: $10 billion of new common stock and $10 billion of bonds in six parts maturing 2029 to 2046, with pricing not yet set. That uses the whole $20 billion cap on extra AI financing through fiscal 2030 that Oracle set the week before. Selling new shares spreads ownership over more shares, so each existing share owns a smaller piece. Oracle had $125.34 billion of notes and borrowings at 31 August against fiscal 2026 revenue of $67.36 billion, and investors worried about its exposure to OpenAI. The stock fell 8.8% to $119.22.

Sources: Reuters · Motley Fool

Apple beats forecasts in its new chief executive's first quarter

A quarterly report is a scorecard compared against analyst expectations. Apple's fiscal fourth quarter, its first under CEO John Ternus, brought revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12%, against $113.39 billion expected. Earnings per share were $2.10 against $1.99 expected, including $0.05 from tariff refunds. iPhone revenue was $57.6 billion and services a record $31.6 billion, while iPad and wearables missed forecasts. The $1,999 iPhone Duo went on sale in October, so it is not in these results, and it is supply-constrained. How the market judges all this is unclear, because after-hours reports disagree: Reuters says up 0.8%, AP flat, Fortune slightly down.
