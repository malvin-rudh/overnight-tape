Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Prices rise when money and credit spent grow faster than output. Gold pays no interest, so lower after-inflation bond returns reduce the income given up by holding it.

What the data say: Three forces matter. First, the real yield fell to 2.89% on 17 Nov, helping gold through that channel. Second, inflation compensation rose to 2.21% on 17 Nov. Third, the broad dollar index was 122.6503 on 13 Nov, a stale input. Normally lower real returns support gold. This time the currency pressure and reported demand for cash complicate that template.

What looks unsustainable: Debt cannot indefinitely outgrow revenue without adjustment. Cuts, restructuring, transfers or monetary expansion shift who bears the cost.

What would change this read: FOMC minutes at 03:00 HKT Thursday could change the expected pace of rate increases. A sustained change in real yields would matter more than hawkish wording alone.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. Cash earns short-term interest and loses buying power if prices rise faster. If debt pressure leads to easing that cuts real returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds depend on inflation and repayment terms, while stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs.

AI and tech

Nvidia revenue doubles, with a larger quarter forecast

A data centre is a building full of computers, and Nvidia sells chips that do calculations for AI. Think of a company supplying equipment to many busy workshops. Nvidia reported third quarter revenue of US$115.8 billion on November 17, more than double a year earlier. Its Data Center business supplied US$107.6 billion of that total, up 110%. Management forecasts US$130 billion in revenue for the next quarter, plus or minus 3%. That quarter has an additional week, and the forecast assumes no Data Center computing revenue from China. How closely actual sales will match the forecast remains open.

Microsoft releases new coding models at Ignite

A coding model takes instructions and information from a software project, then generates or changes code. For example, a developer could ask it to edit a function and explain the change. Microsoft announced MAI Code 2 and Code 2 Flash on November 17 and says both are available in GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry. It positions Code 2 for complex programming work and Flash for faster routine edits and explanations. Microsoft's comparisons of cost and quality come from its own tests. How well either model handles a customer's particular code remains to be established, and generated changes still need checking.
