Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: When spending financed by money and credit outruns production, the price paid per unit can rise. Higher returns on bonds after inflation also raise the opportunity cost of holding gold, which pays no interest.

What the data say: Three forces matter. The US 10-year real yield held at 2.79% on 24 Nov, leaving the return advantage of Treasuries in place. Ten-year market inflation compensation edged down to 2.12% from 2.13%, a small change in the market’s inflation outlook. The dollar eased against a basket of major currencies, which can help gold buyers abroad. Gold still fell in US trading. Reuters reported selling across asset classes, which the yield and currency figures cannot measure.

What looks unsustainable: Rising debt service can crowd out other spending if it grows faster than revenue. Spending cuts, restructuring, transfers and money creation distribute those costs differently.

What would change this read: October PCE and the second Q3 GDP estimate are due at 21:30 HKT today. Cooler inflation with weaker growth could soften the expected rate path. Firm inflation with resilient demand could keep real returns elevated.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. Cash earns short-term interest and loses buying power if prices rise faster. If debt pressure leads to easing that cuts real returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds depend on inflation and repayment terms, while stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs.

AI and tech

Dell reports $22 billion in quarterly AI server sales

An AI server is a computer built to run the calculations that AI needs. Buying one is like equipping a workshop before knowing how much work it will bring in. Dell reported on November 24 that quarterly sales of its AI servers reached US$22 billion, up 291% from a year earlier. Total revenue was US$54.9 billion, more than double the previous year’s quarter. Management also raised its forecast for full year AI server revenue from US$74 billion to US$78 billion. That forecast remains uncertain, and strong equipment sales leave open whether customers will earn back the money they spend.

Sources: Company release · Release syndication

Industrial chips helped power Analog Devices’ record year

A chip company can grow because many different customers buy more equipment. Think of a supplier selling parts to several kinds of workshop, with some serving AI projects and others doing different work. Analog Devices reported on November 24 that annual revenue reached US$15.2 billion, up 38%. It said more than two-thirds of the increase came from markets it classifies as having a focus beyond AI. That share describes the extra sales added during the year. The company also said AI investment helped its data centre and chip testing businesses grow. How much each market will contribute to growth next year remains open.
