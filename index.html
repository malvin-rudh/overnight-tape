Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Gold competes with cash and bonds as a store of wealth. Holding it gives up the real yield, the return after inflation, so a higher real yield makes gold costlier to hold and inflation above yields makes it cheaper.

What the data say: The template says gold gains when bonds' real return falls. Three forces today. First, the real yield eased to 2.64% from 2.66%, still high. Second, a 4.99% 10-year still beats 3.7% CPI. Third, 4.2% unemployment and 194k claims let the Fed stay tight. All lean against gold. The difference: the real yield fell only 2bp and the 2.35% breakeven barely moved, yet spot rose $50 since Tuesday's edition, a rally FXStreet tied to cheaper oil on 27 Oct. Rough gauges.

What looks unsustainable: Federal debt is 6.52 times revenue and interest takes 21.1% of it, like a family whose interest bill eats a fifth of its pay and grows with every rate rise.

What would change this read: An FOMC statement at 02:00 HKT Thu 29 Oct that pushes back on December hikes, or core PCE below the 0.3% m/m forecast (Forex Factory, checked 28 Oct) at 20:30 HKT Thu 29 Oct.

Where this sits in the cycle: Debt is still growing faster than the income that services it: 6.52 times revenue against 6.40 a year ago. The real 10-year yield sits 0.46 points above real growth, against 2.27 points below a year earlier, so the real cost of carrying debt now exceeds the real growth that pays for it, a mark of the late, highly indebted phase. Nominal growth still outruns nominal yields, which keeps the burden manageable for now, though one quarter of GDP is a noisy gauge.

What the framework implies for asset classes: In the framework's logic, cash earns a positive real return while policy stays tight; bonds are exposed if inflation stays above 3%; stocks depend on productivity outrunning higher funding costs; and gold tends to benefit if policy makers later ease and let inflation shrink debts, and to suffer while real yields stay high. These are pressures, not holdings.

AI and tech

Meta launches a stock sale of up to $95 billion for AI

Picture a company asking investors to chip in so it can buy a great many expensive machines. Meta plans to sell up to $95 billion of new shares: about $35 billion now and about $60 billion later, gradually into the market, not before the first quarter of 2027 and with no agreement yet. Part of the money comes from forward sales, so Meta gets paid only when the agreements settle, by November 2027. The filing says only 'general corporate purposes', though Meta reportedly ties it to AI infrastructure. Nvidia says it will invest $3 billion. Will AMD and Broadcom buy too? Unknown: they only signalled non-binding interest, and final pricing is not set.

Sources: Meta SEC filing · Yahoo Finance · MarketScreener

Oracle adds Google, Cerebras and SambaNova chips beside Nvidia

A cloud company rents out computing power, like a power station sells electricity, and the machines are AI chips. Oracle said on Tuesday it is expanding its Nvidia deal to 'multi-gigawatt scale' and adding chips from Google, Cerebras and SambaNova. Training is when an AI model learns; inference is when it answers users. Reuters says Nvidia dominates training while inference is more competitive, and Cerebras and SambaNova focus on inference. Google's own chips, TPUs, reportedly arrive in another company's data centre for the first time. The motive, per Yahoo Finance, is avoiding a single supplier and containing costs. Unknown: deal values, chip quantities, rollout dates, and how Oracle will finance the purchases.

Sources: Reuters via Investing.com · Yahoo Finance
