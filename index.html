Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: When spending financed by money and credit grows faster than the amount sold, prices rise. Gold pays no interest, so higher after-inflation bond yields increase the return an investor gives up by holding it.

What the data say: Three forces carry into Monday. First, October producer prices rose 0.3% on the month, down from a revised 1.4% in September in the 13 Nov report. Second, Friday’s 10-year real yield was 2.91%, giving bonds a larger return advantage over gold. Third, the 10-year inflation compensation gauge held at 2.24% on Friday. Real yields can fall when inflation expectations rise faster than bond yields. On Friday the real yield rose and market inflation compensation held steady. Cash Treasury levels still reflect Friday.

What looks unsustainable: If federal debt continues to outgrow revenue, interest absorbs money that could go to other spending. Cuts, restructuring, transfers and monetary expansion distribute the adjustment differently.

What would change this read: Fed Chair Warsh’s opening remarks at 01:00 HKT Tuesday could change the expected pace of tightening if he discusses monetary policy. The stated topic is technology’s effect on jobs and productivity.

Where this sits in the cycle: The checklist still suggests a highly indebted phase: debt grew relative to output and annual revenue, while foreign Treasury holdings grew more slowly than a year earlier. A household spending more income on interest has less room for other bills. The comparison has limits: the government issues its own currency, and these interest and revenue observations are from different quarters. Negative yield-growth gaps using one annualised quarter do not establish sustainable financing or predict a crisis date.

What the framework implies for asset classes: Positive after-inflation Treasury yields compete with gold. Cash earns short-term interest and loses buying power if prices rise faster. If debt pressure leads to easing that cuts real returns, gold can gain through that channel. Bonds depend on inflation and repayment terms, while stocks depend on productivity and profits outrunning financing costs.

AI and tech

Starship is caught by its launch tower after returning from orbit

To fly the same rocket hardware again, a launch company first has to bring it back and then get it ready for another flight. Recovering it is like getting a plane back to the gate, where it can be checked before taking off again. AP reported that on November 14 US time, the upper stage of SpaceX’s Starship returned from orbit after roughly 22 hours and was caught by the launch tower’s mechanical arms in Texas. It was the 15th full scale Starship test and the second to reach orbit. How quickly and consistently SpaceX can prepare a recovered stage for another flight remains open.

Sources: AP · SpaceX mission plan · 14–15 Nov US dates

Ukraine says satellite service restrictions are hampering air defence

Starshield is a SpaceX service that uses satellites to carry military communications. A terminal is the equipment on the ground that connects to the service, and limits on its operation can affect when a connection is available. For example, a team trying to stop a drone may need to share its position while it is still in flight. On November 15, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said continued restrictions on the operating speed of Starshield terminals along the front and in occupied territory were hurting efforts to intercept Russian drones targeting power substations. AP and BBC reported his assessment. The reports leave open how much the restrictions contributed to missed interceptions.

Sources: AP · Ukraine presidency background · BBC · 14–15 Nov US dates
