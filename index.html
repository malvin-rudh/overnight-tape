AI and tech

IMF flags "circular" AI deals. The IMF counted 14 disclosed deals since 2023 in which cloud giants put money into AI firms that then committed to buy their cloud services and chips (IMF). Reuters reported the IMF's scenario that an AI market correction could cut global GDP 1.1% by 2028.

Applied Materials fell 6.7% after an investor day that set 2030 margin targets (about 54% gross) but gave no revenue forecast (Motley Fool).

Arm v Qualcomm. A Delaware jury found Arm breached its licence on two claims but awarded $2 in total; Arm won the other four (Arm).

Character.AI in bankruptcy. The chatbot start-up filed for Chapter 11, a US court process that lets a firm keep operating while it restructures or sells itself, with Disney the opening bidder at $20m for an IP licence (Tech Startups). ASML reports Q3 results on Wednesday, Amsterdam time.

Explained

Where this goes: why the IMF worries when an investor is also its own customer.

Try first: If you lend a friend money so they can buy your bike, how sure are you of getting paid?

The idea: A sale proves demand only if the buyer's money is really theirs. Picture a bakery lending a café cash that the café spends on the bakery's bread. If the café fails, the bakery loses the loan and the customer at once. The IMF counts 14 deals where big cloud companies put about $300bn of equity and $650bn of debt into AI firms that promised about $1.4tn of future spending, mostly on cloud and chips. Risk managers call this "wrong-way risk": what you are owed gets riskier just as the borrower weakens.

Still unknown: The IMF does not name the 14 deals, so outsiders cannot check which promises are binding.
