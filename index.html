Debt-cycle read

Applies Ray Dalio's published debt-cycle framework to today's data. Not his view, and not endorsed by him.

The mechanics: Gold competes with cash and bonds as a store of wealth. Holding it means giving up the real yield, the return after inflation, so a rising real yield raises gold's cost and inflation running above yields lowers it.

What the data say: The template says gold gains when real returns on bonds fall. Three forces today. First, the real yield rose to 2.66%. Second, bonds still beat inflation: a 5.00% 10-year against 3.7% CPI. Third, unemployment of 4.2% and 194k claims let the Fed keep money tight. All three lean against gold. The difference from the template: oil-driven inflation is pushing hike bets, not easing, so the breakeven sits at only 2.34%. These are rough gauges.

What looks unsustainable: Federal debt is 6.52 times revenue and interest takes 21.1% of it, like a family whose interest bill eats a fifth of its pay and grows with every rate rise.

What would change this read: An FOMC statement at 02:00 HKT Thu 29 Oct that pushes back on December hikes, or core PCE below the 0.3% m/m forecast at 20:30 HKT Thu 29 Oct.

Where this sits in the cycle: Debt is still growing faster than the income that services it: 6.52 times revenue against 6.40 a year ago. The real 10-year yield now sits 0.44 points above real growth, against 2.32 points below a year earlier, so the real cost of carrying debt exceeds the real growth that pays for it, a mark of the late, highly indebted phase. Nominal growth still outruns nominal yields, which keeps the burden manageable for now, though one quarter of GDP is a noisy gauge.

What the framework implies for asset classes: In the framework's logic, cash earns a positive real return while policy stays tight; bonds are exposed if inflation stays above 3%; stocks depend on productivity outrunning higher funding costs; and gold tends to benefit if policy makers later ease and let inflation shrink debts, and to suffer while real yields stay high. These are pressures, not holdings.

AI and tech

SpaceX borrows $40bn for Nvidia chips

Debt is borrowed money with a repayment schedule, and SpaceX says it has closed $40 billion of it to buy Nvidia GPUs, the chips that train and run AI models. Think of buying a delivery fleet instead of renting one: delivery is due in the first half of 2027. Elon Musk credited SpaceX's investment-grade credit rating and revenue from Starlink and rocket launches. He warned that more than 200 "neocloud" firms, which rent computing power to AI developers, could fail or merge, because debt markets have almost stopped lending to companies without major revenue outside AI data centers. What is missing? Interest rate, maturity and lenders were not disclosed, and the closing rests on Musk's own posts.

Sources: TipRanks

Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro, open weights promised

Imagine a hospital with 896 specialists where each patient sees only 16 of them. That is the mixture-of-experts design behind Moonshot AI's Kimi K3.1 Pro, released on 23 October. It builds on Kimi K3, which has about 3 trillion parameters (the adjustable numbers that hold what it learned), but only 16 of its 896 expert sub-networks activate at a time, which cuts running costs. Moonshot reports 65.7% on a coding test, ahead of OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra and slightly behind Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5. Open weights, meaning model files anyone can download, are promised by 4 November. The catch: every benchmark is self-reported and unverified, and cybersecurity scores trail because that data was deliberately removed.

Sources: SiliconANGLE · MarkTechPost

Texas sues OpenAI over ChatGPT

A lawsuit is a written list of accusations that a court must still test. Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton sued OpenAI and Sam Altman on 23 October, alleging ChatGPT was not sufficiently regulated and that Texans were harmed, including minors becoming addicted without parental oversight and vulnerable people being encouraged toward suicide. His office wants an injunction, which is a court order, that would bar minors from ChatGPT and require a court-appointed evaluator to approve any new OpenAI models. Context matters too: Paxton is running for US Senate, a campaign backed by Elon Musk, who owns the rival product Grok. None of the claims are proven, and OpenAI had not yet responded to a request for comment.

Sources: The Texas Tribune via KBTX

Countries pushed to pick an AI bloc

Picture two clubs, each hoping members will shop at its own store. Pax Silica is a US-led group focused on AI supply chains: chips, data centres and high-tech manufacturing. The World AI Cooperation Organization (WAICO) is China-inspired, aims to set AI standards, and had 29 initial founding countries including Kazakhstan, Pakistan and Russia. The US State Department said countries "can't be trusted partners in Pax Silica" while backing a competing vision. Yet Kazakhstan joined both, Indonesia joined WAICO, Singapore is considering it, and Japan, aligned with the US, will not reject cheaper Chinese models for lower-risk uses. A former Kazakh diplomat said "Everyone is hedging." Whether dual membership can last is unknown, and this rests on one AP report.
